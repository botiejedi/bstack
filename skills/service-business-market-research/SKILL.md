# Service Business Market Research

Find practical entry opportunities by comparing actual competing offers, customer evidence, service coverage and delivery economics. Produce a ranked validation decision, not a company directory or a speculative market-size estimate.

## Standing approach

- Prefer complementary work and customer-fit, scheduling or territory gaps over attacking incumbents or competing primarily on price. Investigate referral possibilities without assuming providers want subcontractors.
- Separate verified offers, review allegations, analyst interpretations, modeled costs and untested demand. A proposed improvement is not evidence competitors fail to provide it.
- Preserve existing project decisions and readiness gates. Research can continue while branding or formation is unresolved; do not silently authorize outreach, publication, spending or customer commitments.
- Lead the final answer with the recommendation and decisive counterevidence. Save detailed source-backed findings in the existing project, link follow-up corrections to earlier research, and end with the one input or test that advances the decision.

## When to use

Use for a service-business launch, competitor pricing/tactics analysis, review-based service improvements, complementary referral strategy, or drive-time market expansion.

Do not use as a recurring competitor-news monitor, a legal formation guide, or a substitute for actual customer demand validation.

## Procedure

### 1. Read the existing decision record

Use `read_file` or the vault's read/search tools to find the project brief, prior market report and current decisions. Extract the intended customer, service boundary, actual staffing, spending assumptions, existing candidates and unconfirmed commitments. Label older recorded facts separately from fresh confirmation.

Define the few questions that could change the offer: what job, for whom, at what complete price, on what schedule, with what support afterward. Bound research to the leading named competitors and a small candidate territory set; expand only to resolve a consequential uncertainty.

If a drive limit matters, ask for a departure ZIP or public intersection and likely travel window, not a home address. If unanswered, continue independent research and label any public-landmark routing provisional.

### 2. Inspect the full commercial offer

Load `grounded-citations`. Use `web_search` for discovery and `web_extract` for substantive pages; recover incomplete content with validated direct retrieval or browser interaction. Register URLs at retrieval and preserve the evidence.

Read assessment, pricing/cost FAQ, service process, terms, support FAQ, coverage, takeover and offboarding pages, not just a landing page. Build one row per provider containing:

- Customer/problem segmentation and acquisition entry point.
- What a free assessment actually delivers and what diagnosis costs, if published.
- Rates, minimums, deposits, travel treatment, contract/cancellation terms and exclusions.
- Monitoring versus staffed contact; emergency eligibility; acknowledgement versus onsite arrival; guarantee conditions.
- Change approval, acceptance, documentation, account ownership and handoff.
- Advertised coverage, claimed operating base and independently verified location evidence as distinct fields.

Classify monetary figures before comparing them: selling price, promotional value, platform-license comparison, financing cost, payment timing or insurance limit. An assessment advertised as free with a stated value does not establish a paid assessment rate.

Check deeper terms before proposing uniqueness. Written findings, equipment reuse, owner access, evening work, small projects and no long-term contract may already be offered. Treat unpublished terms as unknown, not refusal or concealment.

### 3. Build a balanced customer-review sample

Match domain, contact details, brand and locality before assigning reviews. Keep related firms separate even if they share a phone number. Do not treat national-brand feedback as locally verified work.

Preserve a structured sample with provider, source URL/platform, retrieval surface, displayed date, full text or excerpt status, visible rating, reviewer label, available provider reply, supported job geography and duplicate/incident grouping. Use `execute_code` to count actual records and reconcile them with declared totals.

Inspect positive counterevidence and provider responses alongside complaints. Distinguish entries from unique reviewers and incidents; two reviews can describe one call. Report inspected sample sizes separately from platform totals. Keep selected-mirror bias and native-platform access gaps visible.

Do not infer the cause of an inaccessible low-star review or describe a company as having no complaints because bodies were unavailable. Preserve both sides of disputed allegations and convert them into proposed operating controls, not misconduct findings or hostile sales copy.

### 4. Test territory against supply and demand

For each corridor, obtain an actual business-community or economic-development source and a relevant supplier source beyond the original competitor set. General IT firms, repair shops, regional installers and ISP-managed services can be substitutes without matching the intended vendor branding.

Treat a chamber as a discovery channel, development activity as a possible trigger, a city page as advertised coverage and a regional dispatch statement as a delivery model. None proves a buyer pipeline, local staffing, refusal of small jobs or an unserved locality.

Load `maps` before routing. Validate settlement name, county and feature type before accepting coordinates. Preserve responses and distinguish baseline routing from live traffic. Do not combine communities into one delivery route merely because they were paired in the research request.

Apply `references/territory-and-pricing.md` when geography affects the recommendation. Rank conditional tests only where evidence yields a concrete proposition to compare; omit an unsupported third choice rather than inventing a complete ranking.

### 5. Design and cost the candidate offer

Define a small deliverable, acceptance result, time/visit cap, included travel, exclusions, change-approval threshold and actual support availability. Compare the complete customer commitment, not unrelated hourly figures or bundled hardware projects.

Calculate all founder person-hours, vehicle costs, overhead assumptions and callback sensitivity with `execute_code` or `terminal`. Label modeled prices as hypotheses, not market rates or achieved margins. Keep founder time value separate from cash spending and take-home pay. Use the territory/pricing reference for the calculation sequence.

Keep discounted pilots as delivery experiments. Require an actual undiscounted purchase before treating willingness to pay the proposed normal price as observed.

### 6. Propose a complementary validation test

Check in-house/no-subcontractor statements before recommending white-label delivery. A co-managed client offering does not establish an open partner program. Treat adjacent referral sources as hypotheses until a provider agrees to a defined relationship.

After the project's readiness/outreach gates, propose a small permission-based sample using one exact offer, truthful sample deliverable, disclosed complete price and realistic appointment window. Ask about the last real fault, existing provider, actual quoted delay/trip charge/minimum/refusal and whether the proposed job would be purchased separately. Distinguish observed obstacles from assumptions.

Use actual bookings, measured delivery time and callbacks as continuation criteria. Do not equate compliments, clicks, chamber membership or free pilots with paid demand. Preserve the incumbent's authorized role and make ownership, scope, customer consent and escalation explicit.

### 7. Verify and deliver

When delegating, split evidence work into nonoverlapping provider groups or source types. Require bounded scope, report paths, captures, structured review samples, separate citation ledgers and explicit unknowns. Verify returned artifacts before synthesis.

Keep worker citation IDs local. Register fetched URLs into the synthesis ledger and map IDs before merging prose, or retain separately numbered appendices. Preserve quotations and their original evidence paths; do not reconstruct evidence from a worker's paraphrase.

Run the citation skill's evidence verification, then manually check claim entailment. A source with an attached quote does not automatically support every sentence citing it. Reconcile review counts programmatically and check that every territory recommendation includes supplier counterevidence.

Deliver one strategy memo and, when needed, one territory/pricing note plus a calculated export. Add a follow-up pointer where stronger research corrects the earlier interpretation; avoid one vault note per worker. In chat, report the recommendation, strongest counterevidence, candidate tests, price assumptions, saved artifacts and one remaining decision.

## Completion checks

- Competitor claims are attributed and unpublished commercial terms remain unknown.
- Review identities, dates, sample boundaries, duplicates and replies are preserved.
- Proposed gaps survived a search for contrary provider offers and alternative suppliers.
- Travel uses a verified or explicitly provisional origin and identified routing limitations.
- Prices include full labor/travel assumptions and callback sensitivity without pretending demand is proven.
- The next test has a paid outcome and stop condition, not an ever-growing research backlog.
